# Holiday Planning Guide for Retail & CPG

## Planning Timeline: Start 16–20 Weeks Out

Effective holiday execution begins long before the first shopper walks through the door. Best-in-class retailers kick off their holiday planning 16 to 20 weeks before peak season, aligning cross-functional teams around a unified calendar. Merchandising, supply chain, marketing, and store operations should finalize their joint plan by mid-July for the winter holiday season. This lead time allows for purchase-order placement with overseas suppliers, fixture procurement, and creative development for seasonal campaigns. A well-structured planning timeline also leaves room for scenario planning — modeling best-case, expected, and conservative demand curves so the organization can pivot quickly if early indicators shift.

## Display Strategy: Build Visibility by Early October

Holiday displays should be set no later than the first week of October for categories that benefit from extended selling seasons, such as confectionery, gifting, and home décor. Endcap and lobby placements drive the highest incremental lift — on average 30–40 % above in-aisle placement — so securing premium real estate early is critical. Layered display strategies work well: start with "harvest and gratitude" themes in October, transition to gifting and entertaining by mid-November, and shift to value-packs and last-minute stocking stuffers in the final two weeks before Christmas. Ensure plan-o-grams include clear price communication and limited-time-offer call-outs to create urgency.

## Inventory: The Build-Peak-Clear Model

A proven approach to holiday inventory is the build-peak-clear model. During the *build* phase (8–10 weeks out), gradually increase forward stock of key holiday SKUs, targeting 1.2× to 1.5× normal weeks of supply. The *peak* phase (the two to three weeks surrounding the holiday) demands aggressive in-stock discipline — out-of-stocks during peak can cost a retailer 2–4 % of category revenue. Finally, the *clear* phase begins the day after the holiday: implement pre-planned markdown cadences (typically 25 %, then 50 %, then 75 % off) to liquidate seasonal inventory within 10–14 days, protecting gross margin while reclaiming shelf space for the next seasonal set. Demand-sensing tools that incorporate POS velocity, weather data, and regional trends can sharpen forecasts significantly during each phase.

## Staffing and Execution

Seasonal staffing should ramp in three waves: an early wave of experienced associates for display setup and training (6 weeks out), a main hiring wave for peak coverage (4 weeks out), and a flex pool of on-call associates for the final surge. Cross-train seasonal hires on both front-end and floor replenishment tasks so labor can be deployed where it is needed most on any given day. Daily huddles during peak weeks — even 10-minute stand-ups — keep teams aligned on priorities such as hot-item replenishment, promotional changeovers, and customer-service flash points. Investing in staffing depth during the holiday window consistently correlates with higher conversion rates and improved customer satisfaction scores.

## Post-Season Review

Within two weeks of the holiday close, conduct a structured post-mortem covering sales vs. plan, gross margin performance, in-stock rates, markdown effectiveness, and customer feedback themes. Capture lessons learned in a shared playbook so the next planning cycle starts from a stronger baseline. Compare regional performance to identify best-practice stores whose tactics can be scaled across the fleet. This continuous-improvement loop is what separates retailers who merely survive the holidays from those who win them.
