# Promotion Effectiveness Report — FY 2024 Analysis

## Executive Summary

This report evaluates the effectiveness of promotional programs executed across the retail portfolio during FY 2024, covering 1,247 distinct promotional events across 14 regions. Overall promotional spend totaled $18.4 M, generating an estimated incremental revenue of $42.1 M — a blended ROI of 2.29×. While headline performance improved 8 % year over year, significant variance exists across promotion types, regions, and timing windows. The findings below are intended to guide promotional investment decisions for FY 2025, helping the team double down on high-performing tactics and reduce spend on programs that failed to deliver above the 1.5× ROI threshold.

## Promotion Type Analysis

**Temporary Price Reductions (TPR / Discount):** TPRs accounted for 42 % of total promo spend and delivered a median ROI of 2.1×. Shallow discounts (10–15 % off) on high-velocity items outperformed deep discounts (25 %+) on slower movers, largely because deep cuts attracted cherry-pickers without driving basket expansion. **BOGO / Multi-Buy:** Buy-one-get-one and multi-buy offers represented 22 % of spend with a 2.5× median ROI — the highest of any tactic — driven by strong pantry-loading behavior in center-store categories like snacks and canned goods. However, post-promotion sales dips averaged 12 % in the two weeks following BOGO events, indicating forward-buy effects. **Display-Only (no price change):** Secondary displays without a price incentive generated a 1.8× ROI, confirming that incremental visibility alone drives meaningful lift (typically 18–25 % above baseline). This tactic is especially cost-effective because it avoids margin erosion. **Digital Coupons / Personalized Offers:** Digital promotions delivered a 2.3× ROI with the added benefit of precise attribution via loyalty-card redemption data. Personalized offers targeting lapsed buyers in a category showed 35 % higher redemption rates than blanket digital coupons. **Bundling / Cross-Merch:** Bundled offers (e.g., chips + dip, wine + cheese) posted a 1.9× ROI. While not the highest-performing tactic in isolation, bundles excelled at increasing basket size — shoppers exposed to bundles had an average transaction that was $4.20 higher than matched control shoppers.

## Regional Patterns

The Southeast and Midwest regions consistently outperformed on promotional ROI (2.5× and 2.4× respectively), driven by higher baseline brand loyalty and lower competitive promotional intensity. The Northeast delivered the lowest average ROI (1.7×), attributable to aggressive competitor matching that compressed margins. West Coast markets showed the strongest digital coupon adoption, with 28 % of total redemptions occurring via mobile — nearly double the national average. These regional differences underscore the importance of tailoring the promotional mix by geography rather than applying a one-size-fits-all national plan.

## Timing Insights

Promotions launched on Thursdays and Fridays generated 14 % higher incremental lift than those starting on Mondays or Tuesdays, aligning with the weekend stock-up shopping mission. Seasonal peaks — back-to-school (August), Thanksgiving (November), and Super Bowl (late January) — produced the three highest-ROI windows of the year, each exceeding 3.0× ROI. Conversely, the post-holiday January clearance period and mid-summer (late June through mid-July) were the weakest windows, with average ROIs below 1.5×. The data strongly suggests shifting discretionary promotional dollars toward the proven high-lift windows and reducing investment during historically weak periods, reserving those windows for display-only tactics that maintain visibility without margin sacrifice.
